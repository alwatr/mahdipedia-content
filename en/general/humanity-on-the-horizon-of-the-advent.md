**Humanity on the Horizon of the Advent**

**Reconsidering the Truth of Humanity in the Mahdist Call**

**From Human Dignity to Human Flourishing in the Light of the Imamate of Imam al-Mahdi (may God hasten his reappearance)**

**A Qur’anic, Hadith-Based, and Social Article**

**Abstract**

The idea of Mahdism offers a clear vision of the future of humanity and human society. In this vision, the advent of Imam al-Mahdi (may God hasten his reappearance) is not merely a political event or a transfer of power; it opens a horizon for the revival of justice, the spread of knowledge, the reform of social relations, and the flourishing of human potential. The Noble Qur’an presents the human being as endowed with dignity, free will, responsibility, and the capacity for growth: a being who can follow the path of servitude to God and development, or drift away from their true nature through negligence and injustice. Drawing on Qur’anic and hadith foundations, this article examines the relationship between the Islamic understanding of the human being and the Mahdist call. It shows that justice, knowledge, ethics, security, and spirituality in the promised society are essential conditions for preserving human dignity and fostering human growth. It also emphasizes that associating a movement with Mahdism does not, by itself, establish its authenticity; every claim must be assessed against sound religious standards and its ethical and social consequences.

Keywords: Qur’anic anthropology, human dignity, Mahdism, justice, ethics, promised society, Imamate.

1. **Introduction: The Human Being as the Central Concern of Mahdist Transformation**

To ask about humanity’s future is, in fact, to ask about the human being’s destiny: Where is humanity heading? What capacities lie within human beings? And what conditions can enable those capacities to flourish? The idea of Mahdism offers a hopeful answer to these questions. From this perspective, human history has not reached a dead end, and injustice, ignorance, and corruption are not humanity’s inevitable fate. A more just future is possible: one in which divine guidance and the leadership of the Imam create the conditions for ordering individual and social life.

Yet if Mahdism is reduced solely to the signs of the advent or the structure of a future government, an important part of its meaning is overlooked. A just government matters because it frees people from oppression and deprivation and expands their opportunities for growth, informed choice, and a dignified life. Human beings and their dignity therefore occupy a central place in understanding the social and ethical implications of the Mahdist call.

1. **The Human Being in the Qur’an: Dignity, Free Will, and Responsibility**

The Noble Qur’an speaks of human dignity: “We have certainly honored the children of Adam” (al-Isra’, 17:70). This verse affirms a fundamental status for the human race. Such dignity provides a basis for respecting people and refraining from humiliating, oppressing, or exploiting them. Human dignity, however, does not mean that all individuals are equal in their degrees of virtue and piety. Alongside its affirmation of human dignity, the Qur’an identifies piety as the measure of spiritual distinction: “Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you” (al-Hujurat, 49:13).

In the Qur’an, the human being possesses free will and responsibility. The account of Adam’s creation and his appointment as a vicegerent on earth (al-Baqarah, 2:30) points to humanity’s distinctive capacities and responsibilities. The verse concerning the Trust (al-Ahzab, 33:72) likewise portrays the weight of human moral and spiritual responsibility. From this perspective, a human being is not powerless or wholly determined by circumstances, but a responsible agent whose choices shape both personal character and society.

The Qur’an also speaks of the divinely given primordial nature of the human being: “the natural disposition of Allah upon which He has created mankind” (al-Rum, 30:30). This innate disposition is an inner orientation toward truth and goodness, although habit, ignorance, selfishness, and an unhealthy environment may obscure it. The mission of the prophets and the guidance of the Imams can be understood in relation to this innate capacity: reminding people of the truth and helping them follow the path of growth.

1. **The Crisis of Humanity in the Age of Occultation**

In every era, people may become distanced from their true nature. The signs of this estrangement are not limited to major political and economic crises. They can also appear in everyday conduct: disregard for others’ rights, lying and betrayal, contempt for the vulnerable, and the reduction of human relationships to purely self-interested transactions. War, discrimination, imposed poverty, corruption, and deprivation are also among the forces that threaten human dignity.

All the world’s problems should not be reduced to a single cause, nor should every social phenomenon be explained through one moral label. Nevertheless, it is clear that reform of social structures cannot be sustained without reforming human attitudes and behavior. Just as moral exhortation alone cannot replace institutional justice and just laws, structural reform cannot achieve its full result without individual responsibility, honesty, and moral conscience.

Therefore, awaiting the Mahdi should not lead to passivity or indifference toward human suffering. Those who hope for a future filled with justice must also, in the present and to the extent of their ability, oppose oppression, discrimination, deception, and irresponsibility, and act more justly within their families and communities.

1. **Cultivating the Human Being in the Mahdist Call**

The Mahdist call can be understood as an invitation to rebuild both the human being and society. In this process, people are not merely recipients of protection and governance; they are also addressed by guidance and are agents in realizing values. A desirable society is not built through commands and laws alone. It needs people who understand justice, seek the truth, take responsibility for the rights of others, and demonstrate practical commitment to ethical values.

Reports concerning the era of the advent emphasize the expansion of justice and the emergence of humanity’s intellectual capacities. In a well-known report from Imam al-Baqir (peace be upon him) concerning al-Qa’im (may God hasten his reappearance), God is said to fill the earth through him with equity and justice after it has been filled with oppression and tyranny. This theme presents justice as a central characteristic of Mahdist governance. Some reports also speak of the spread of knowledge in the era of the advent; such reports should be assessed individually in light of their chains of transmission and wording.

Mahdist cultivation of the human being does not mean ignoring differences or imposing superficial uniformity on people. Rather, it means creating conditions in which sound human capacities can develop and individuals can live responsibly through knowledge, justice, and spirituality.

1. **Mahdist Justice: A Foundation for Preserving Human Dignity**

Justice is one of the clearest characteristics of the promised government. It is not merely an individual virtue or moral recommendation; it must be present in law, the judiciary, the economy, the distribution of opportunities, and social relations. Whenever people’s rights are violated because of power, wealth, connections, or social status, human dignity is harmed. Mahdist justice should therefore be understood in connection with restoring rights, removing oppression, and preventing illegitimate domination.

Justice also means respecting the rights of those who disagree with us or differ from us intellectually or socially. If justice is demanded only for like-minded people and close associates, we have not yet approached its full meaning. A culture of awaiting the Imam should cultivate fairness in judgment, fidelity when conveying others’ words, and avoidance of slander and humiliation.

At the same time, detailed claims about the future’s legal and economic structures should not be attributed to religion without clear evidence. The principles that can be affirmed with greater confidence include rejecting oppression, respecting rights, establishing equity, and judging fairly. Identifying the mechanisms for implementing these principles requires separate argument and investigation.

1. **The Mahdist Society: A Society of Knowledge, Ethics, and Solidarity**

A society that preserves human dignity needs a range of supporting values and institutions. First, knowledge and learning must spread, because ignorance and misinformation can create conditions for deception, fanaticism, and oppression. Second, ethics must shape individual and social relationships. Honesty, keeping promises, trustworthiness, compassion, and respect for others’ rights are foundations of social trust.

Third, the family, as one of the most important environments for human development, should be characterized by affection, security, respect, and mutual responsibility. Fourth, the deprived and vulnerable must not be excluded from society’s concern. A just society measures progress not only by wealth and power, but also by people’s quality of life and their access to fair opportunities.

Fifth, spirituality must be connected to social responsibility. Religious devotion that leads to indifference toward the suffering of others is incompatible with the Mahdist commitment to justice. By contrast, spirituality that moves people toward service, fairness, and goodwill can become an effective force for social reform.

1. **The Responsibility of Those Awaiting the Imam toward Humanity**

Awaiting the Imam is not merely an emotional state or a prediction of the time of the advent; in the religious tradition, it is connected with preparedness, reform, and responsibility. Those who await the Imam can begin with small but consistent actions: respecting family members’ rights, acting honestly at work, avoiding rumors and slander, defending what is right fairly, helping people in need, and respecting human dignity.

At the social level, responsible anticipation requires people not to remain indifferent to corruption and discrimination. Yet they must not resort to lies, unjustified violence, or injustice in the pursuit of reform. A sacred goal does not justify an improper method. If the society being anticipated is to be founded on justice, the conduct of those awaiting it today should also be consistent with justice as far as possible.

One test of sincerity in awaiting the Imam, then, is how a person treats those who lack the power to retaliate or defend themselves: family members, colleagues, subordinates, people in need, and even ideological opponents. A claim of love for the Imam gains greater meaning and credibility when it becomes respect, fairness, and service in everyday conduct.

1. **Assessing Mahdist Claims by Standards of Authenticity**

Throughout history, individuals and movements have emerged with various claims concerning Mahdism. Merely using terms such as the advent, the Yamani, designation by succession, or a connection with the Imam is not enough to prove the truth of a particular call. A religious claim must be assessed through reliable evidence. The burden of proof cannot be replaced by emotional enthusiasm, dreams, unsupported reports, or repetition of the claim.

At least several questions should be asked when evaluating any movement: Are its foundations consistent with the Qur’an and reliable Sunnah? Have the reports it cites been examined in terms of transmission, wording, meaning, and historical context? Do its distinctive claims have clear and assessable evidence? Are its conduct and methods consistent with ethical principles and the pursuit of justice? Does it encourage followers to investigate and ask questions, or does it fear criticism and scrutiny?

Ethical conduct alone, however, is not conclusive proof that a doctrinal claim is true; likewise, the misconduct of some followers does not, without further examination, invalidate every foundation of an idea. A fair assessment must evaluate theoretical evidence and practical consequences in their proper place. The authenticity of a Mahdist call cannot be established through unsupported claims or the exploitation of people’s emotions.

1. **Conclusion: The Advent of the Mahdi as a Horizon for Human Flourishing**

In the light of the Qur’an and Mahdist teachings, the human being is endowed with dignity, free will, and responsibility, as well as the capacity to move toward perfection. Yet this potential cannot properly flourish in an environment dominated by oppression, ignorance, discrimination, and selfishness. The promise of the awaited society is the promise of an equitable order guided by divine direction, in which the conditions for human intellectual, ethical, spiritual, and social development are more fully established.

From this perspective, the grandeur of the advent is not confined to the reach of government or a change in power relations. Its true grandeur is to be found in the revival of justice, the spread of knowledge, the preservation of human dignity, and growth in servitude to God. Awaiting the Imam also rises above the level of slogans when it affects the personal and social conduct of those who await him.

Thus, the Mahdist call may be described as an invitation for human beings to return to their divinely grounded nature: the human being is not an instrument of power or wealth, but a bearer of dignity and a being responsible before God, oneself, and others. The more today’s society advances in justice, honesty, compassion, and responsibility, the closer it comes to the fundamental values that occupy a central place in the ideal of the Mahdist society.
