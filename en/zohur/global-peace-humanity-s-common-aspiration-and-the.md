From Dialogue Among Nations to the Horizon of Global Justice in the Era of the Advent

In Commemoration of 24 October: United Nations Day

**Abstract**

Global peace is one of humanity’s oldest aspirations. Despite the expansion of communication and the emergence of international institutions, it continues to face obstacles such as war, discrimination, poverty, power-driven competition, and mistrust among nations. The United Nations was established to provide a framework for international cooperation and dialogue. United Nations Day, observed on 24 October—the anniversary of the entry into force of the UN Charter—offers an opportunity to reconsider the shared responsibility of nations to preserve international peace and security. Mahdist thought presents a horizon that goes beyond the mere cessation of conflicts: a world in which justice, human dignity, security, and divine guidance occupy a central place in individual and social life. Without equating international institutions with the promised government, this article examines points of convergence between the aspiration for global peace and Mahdist teachings. It explains how awaiting the advent can give deeper meaning to people’s present-day responsibility to promote dialogue, fairness, coexistence, and justice.

**Keywords:** Global peace, United Nations, Mahdism, Imam al-Mahdi, global justice, human dignity, awaiting the advent.

1. **Introduction: Peace, an Aspiration Beyond Borders**

Throughout history, human beings have paid a heavy price for war, violence, fanaticism, and destructive rivalries. Every generation has experienced, in one way or another, that insecurity is not confined to battlefields. War tears families apart, destroys opportunities for education and progress, damages economies and public trust, and leaves lasting consequences in the lives of nations. The aspiration for peace and peaceful coexistence is therefore not the concern of one nation or culture alone; it is a universal human aspiration.

Yet lasting peace means more than the absence of war. A society may appear to be free of armed conflict while discrimination, repression, imposed poverty, deprivation, and violations of human dignity continue. Sustainable peace must therefore be accompanied by justice, security, mutual respect, and the opportunity for people to enjoy their fundamental rights.

1. **The United Nations: An Effort toward Global Dialogue and Cooperation**

The United Nations was established after the devastating experience of the Second World War, with the hope of preventing similar tragedies from recurring. The UN Charter was signed in San Francisco on 26 June 1945 and entered into force on 24 October of that year, after the required conditions had been met. For this reason, 24 October is observed annually as United Nations Day.

One of the organization’s central principles is that disagreements among states should, as far as possible, be addressed through negotiation, cooperation, and legal mechanisms rather than the use of force. The United Nations works in areas including international peace and security, cooperation in addressing economic and social issues, humanitarian assistance, and the protection of human rights. Nevertheless, it is not a flawless institution, nor does it possess unlimited authority. Its political structure, competing interests among powerful states, and practical constraints sometimes prevent it from fully achieving its stated objectives.

Despite these limitations, dialogue and cooperation among nations remain valuable principles. Dialogue does not mean ignoring differences; it is an effort to foster mutual understanding, reduce misunderstandings, identify shared solutions, and prevent disagreements from turning into hostility and violence.

1. **Can Global Peace Be Achieved through Political Agreements Alone?**

Political and legal agreements are essential for containing conflicts, but they are not sufficient on their own to build lasting peace. If injustice, humiliation, discrimination, poverty, and exploitation remain embedded in different areas of life, the roots of resentment and violence will not disappear. Enduring peace requires a culture of respect, commitment to justice, responsibility, and trust.

Nor can we expect historical, cultural, religious, and political differences to disappear easily. The realistic goal is not to eliminate differences, but to establish an order in which differences are not used as excuses for depriving people of their rights, humiliating them, or resorting to violence. Just coexistence means that individuals and nations, while retaining their identities and beliefs, recognize one another’s rights and remain committed to fair and peaceful ways of resolving disputes.

1. **The Mahdist Horizon: Peace Founded on Justice and Guidance**

Mahdist thought does not confine humanity’s future to the existing state of affairs. It speaks of a world in which justice occupies a central place. In Islamic narrations, the establishment of equity and justice in the era of the advent is presented as one of the most prominent features of the government of Imam al-Mahdi (may God hasten his noble advent). The well-known statement, “He will fill the earth with equity and justice just as it had been filled with oppression and injustice,” offers an image of a comprehensive transformation in human and social relations, centered on ending the domination of oppression and establishing justice.

Within this horizon, peace is not merely the silencing of weapons or a temporary halt to hostilities. True peace becomes meaningful when people’s rights are protected, security is available to all, double standards and unjust discrimination are removed, and power is placed in the service of truth and justice. Mahdist justice can therefore be understood as a foundation for a deeper and more sustainable peace.

The Qur’an also emphasizes the connection between social reform and justice. The command to uphold justice and excellence in Qur’an 16:90, together with calls for reconciliation and peacemaking in several other verses, demonstrates that a religious society cannot remain indifferent to conflict, oppression, or the violation of human rights. In the Mahdist vision, these values are manifested on a comprehensive global scale.

1. **The Unity of Humanity Does Not Mean Erasing Differences**

An important distinction in discussions of global peace is the difference between unity and enforced uniformity. Nations possess diverse languages, cultures, historical experiences, and traditions. The Qur’an presents this diversity as one of the signs of divine wisdom: “We made you peoples and tribes so that you may come to know one another” (Qur’an 49:13).

From this perspective, differences can provide opportunities for mutual understanding and cooperation rather than justifications for superiority or hostility. Unity in the Mahdist horizon should not be interpreted as the erasure of cultural identities or the coercion of everyone into a single way of thinking. What matters is the establishment of a just order in which people’s dignity, rights, and security are protected, and differences are managed through principled and fair means.

1. **The Relationship between the United Nations and the Mahdist Ideal: Shared Concerns, Different Foundations and Roles**

At the level of general concerns, points of convergence can be found between international efforts to reduce war and Mahdist thought. These include opposition to war and destruction, the importance of cooperation, concern for collective security, and hope for a better future for humanity. However, the United Nations should not be equated with the global government of Imam al-Mahdi (may God hasten his noble advent). The United Nations is an intergovernmental institution that operates under a charter and agreements among states, and it faces political constraints and competing interests. By contrast, according to Shi‘i belief, the promised government represents the fulfillment of divine promise through the leadership of the infallible Imam and the establishment of comprehensive justice.

The proper relationship between the two, therefore, is neither complete equivalence nor substitution. Rather, one may speak of convergence in certain human aspirations. Efforts to encourage dialogue, reduce tensions, and promote global cooperation can be valuable in today’s world without being regarded as the full realization of Mahdist society. Conversely, belief in the advent should not become an excuse for indifference toward institutions and practical efforts that seek to reduce human suffering.

1. **The Responsibility of Those Awaiting the Imam in Building a Culture of Peace**

Awaiting the advent is not simply looking toward the future; it is a living and responsible orientation that should influence a person’s conduct today. Anyone who hopes for a world founded on justice and human dignity cannot remain indifferent to humiliation, dishonesty, unfairness, or the violation of others’ rights in personal life. A culture of peace begins at home, at school, in the workplace, and in social relationships.

Those awaiting the Imam can make a genuine contribution to peace by practicing respectful dialogue, listening to opposing views, avoiding rumors and hate speech, defending the rights of others fairly, helping those affected by hardship, and participating in charitable activities. At the social and global levels, supporting peaceful solutions, humanitarian cooperation, and respect for the rights of nations is consistent with the spirit of religious responsibility.

Peacebuilding, however, does not mean remaining silent in the face of oppression. Defending the oppressed and resisting aggression and injustice may be necessary. Such resistance should, as far as possible, be grounded in truth, ethics, proportionality, and respect for human dignity. The goal is to build a just world, not to reproduce a cycle of violence and revenge.

1. **From a Global Ideal to Everyday Action**

If discussion of global peace is not connected to practical conduct and responsibility, it may become a beautiful but ineffective slogan. Every person, according to their position and abilities, can help create a safer and more humane environment: within the family by resolving disagreements through dialogue; in society by respecting civic rights; in digital spaces by acting responsibly when sharing information; and in intercultural relationships by respecting differences and avoiding prejudice.

Educational, cultural, and religious institutions can also help raise a generation that remains committed to its identity and beliefs while developing the ability to engage in dialogue, cooperate, and respect others. Such a generation understands peace not as a sign of weakness, but as the outcome of ethical and social maturity, and regards justice as a condition for its endurance.

1. **Conclusion: Global Peace, Humanity’s Common Aspiration and the Bright Promise of Mahdism**

United Nations Day provides an opportunity to recall that the destinies of nations are interconnected and that many global challenges require solutions based on cooperation and shared responsibility. Despite its achievements and limitations, the United Nations symbolizes the international community’s effort to replace war and confrontation with dialogue and cooperation.

Mahdist thought places this aspiration within a deeper horizon. In Shi‘i belief, the advent of Imam al-Mahdi (may God hasten his noble advent) is the promise of a world in which justice becomes widespread and people gain the opportunity to live under the protection of guidance, security, and dignity. This horizon reminds us that lasting peace cannot be complete without justice, and that genuine unity is not achieved by erasing differences, but by safeguarding rights and practicing mutual respect.

The connection between United Nations Day and Mahdist thought should therefore be sought in a shared responsibility: working to reduce human suffering, promoting dialogue, safeguarding the dignity of nations, and standing fairly against oppression. Awaiting the advent can begin to have meaning today, when every person accepts a share of responsibility, within their capacity, for building a more peaceful and just world.
