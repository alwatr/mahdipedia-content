Introduction

One of the most fundamental theological questions concerning humanity’s future is this: After the passing of Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him and his family), was humanity left without divine guidance throughout its long historical journey? Is it conceivable that God would call human beings toward perfection and show them the path of guidance, yet leave them without a divinely appointed proof and leader at the most critical stages of history?

This question leads us to one of the most fundamental principles of Shi‘i belief:

The earth is never devoid of a Proof of God.

According to this belief, Imamate does not come to an end with the conclusion of prophethood. Rather, divine guidance continues through Imamate, and in our present age, it is embodied in the sacred existence of Imam Mahdi (may Allah hasten his noble return).

But if the existence of an Imam is necessary in every age, why is Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) hidden from the sight of the general public? What is the relationship between his Occultation and the guidance of humanity? And how can his worldwide uprising fulfill the long-standing aspiration of the prophets: the establishment of justice and the sovereignty of truth?

To answer these questions, we must consider three interconnected truths: the necessity of the Imam’s existence, the continuation of the Divine Proof during the period of Occultation, and the promise of Imam Mahdi’s worldwide uprising.

1. Why Can Humanity Never Do Without an Imam?

To attain perfection, human beings need more than reason and empirical knowledge. They also need a clear understanding of their ultimate destination, a reliable standard for distinguishing truth from falsehood, and a trustworthy model to guide them along the path of devotion to God.

Human reason is one of God’s greatest blessings and an essential means of acquiring knowledge. Nevertheless, differences in interpretation, the limitations of human understanding, the influence of personal interests, and changing values demonstrate that people continually need a standard that transcends individual and collective desires, enabling them to recognize the truth and implement it justly.

According to the Shi‘i perspective, the All-Wise God created humanity for guidance and the attainment of perfection and did not abandon human beings to themselves. The prophets showed humanity the path of guidance, and following the final Prophet, Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him and his family), Imamate assumes the responsibility of continuing divine guidance.

An Imam is not merely a religious scholar or a social administrator. Rather, he is the Proof of God, an authoritative interpreter of religion, and a leader whom, according to Shi‘i belief, God has appointed to guide humanity. Therefore, the necessity of Imamate is not limited to governing society; it is also connected to the very principle of divine guidance and the continuation of authoritative religious leadership.

The Holy Qur’an states:

“On the Day when We will call every people with their Imam.” (Al-Isra, 17:71)

This verse highlights the significance of the Imam in relation to human beings. Although the verse alone does not establish every detail of the Shi‘i doctrine of Imamate, it demonstrates that Imamate is neither a marginal nor an insignificant matter in the Qur’anic worldview.

Furthermore, narrations from the Ahl al-Bayt (peace be upon them) emphasize that the earth is never without a Divine Proof. A narration from Imam al-Rida (peace be upon him) states:

«لَوْ خَلَتِ الْأَرْضُ طَرْفَةَ عَيْنٍ مِنْ حُجَّةٍ لَسَاخَتْ بِأَهْلِهَا.»

“If the earth were to remain without a Proof of God, even for the blink of an eye, it would collapse with its inhabitants.”

This teaching appears in Shi‘i hadith literature and expresses the profound connection between the system of divine guidance and the existence of God’s Proof.

Accordingly, Imamate cannot be regarded merely as an institution belonging to the past, whose role ended with the passing of Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him and his family). If divine guidance is essential for humanity, the continued existence of the Divine Proof is an integral part of this theological framework.

2. From the Manifest Imam to the Hidden Imam: Does Occultation Mean the Imam Does Not Exist?

Once the necessity of an Imam in every age is acknowledged, a more pressing question arises: If an Imam must exist, why are many people unable to reach him directly?

The first point to clarify is the distinction between the Imam’s existence and his public appearance. Occultation does not mean that the Imam does not exist. Rather, it means that he is not publicly manifest to the general population and that ordinary, universal access to him is unavailable.

Throughout history, the existence of a reality has not always meant that it is visible to everyone. This comparison is intended only to clarify the distinction between existence and appearance.

According to Shi‘i theology, Imam Mahdi (may Allah hasten his noble return) is the living Imam and the Divine Proof of our present age. His Occultation does not abolish the principle of Imamate, just as his lack of public appearance does not mean that humanity no longer needs divine guidance.

This brings us to a fundamental principle:

The Occultation of the Imam does not mean the suspension of divine guidance.

The Holy Qur’an describes the mission of the prophets as follows:

“We certainly sent Our messengers with clear proofs, and We sent down with them the Book and the Balance, so that people may uphold justice.” (Al-Hadid, 57:25)

This verse demonstrates that divine guidance calls upon human beings to accept responsibility and establish justice. During the period of Occultation, people remain responsible for religious commitment, recognizing the truth, following divine commandments, and striving to reform society.

Therefore, Occultation must never become an excuse for abandoning responsibilities or neglecting reason, ethics, and religious duties.

3. The Wisdom of Occultation: Why Is the Imam Present Among People Without Publicly Appearing?

Islamic sources discuss various reasons and aspects of the Occultation of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him). However, a distinction must be made between the wisdoms mentioned in narrations and the complete philosophy of Occultation. We cannot claim that every dimension of this matter is fully understood.

Some narrations indicate that the complete reason for the Occultation has not been disclosed to humanity. In addition, Mahdavi teachings consider the preservation of the Imam’s life, unfavorable social and political circumstances, and the absence of the necessary conditions for fulfilling his worldwide mission among the factors relevant to understanding the Occultation.

Nevertheless, Occultation should not be reduced to a single historical or political cause. In Shi‘i theology, belief in Occultation is connected to God’s knowledge and wisdom, even though we may be unable to comprehend all its dimensions.

More importantly, Occultation does not signify approval of the existing state of the world. Injustice, discrimination, corruption, and deviation may continue despite the existence of the Hidden Imam. His existence, however, does not mean that he is required to establish his worldwide government without the necessary conditions or divine permission.

Occultation should therefore be understood as a period in which the Divine Proof exists, while his public appearance and comprehensive uprising have not yet taken place.

During this period, human beings face genuine responsibilities. Will they remain committed to the truth when they lack direct access to the Imam? Do they seek justice only when it serves their own interests, or do they uphold it in their personal and social lives as well? Do they reduce waiting to a passing emotion, or transform it into personal reform and social responsibility?

These questions demonstratethat Occultation is not merely a discussion about the Imam’s circumstances. It is also a discussion about the quality of human life and the responsibilities of humanity.

4. The Hidden Imam and a Society That Still Needs Guidance

Some people may wonder: If the Imam is not publicly accessible, what role does his existence play in human life?

This question arises when Imamate is reduced to physical presence and the direct administration of society. In the Shi‘i understanding, however, the Imam’s position extends beyond these functions.

The Imam is the Proof of God and the focal point of the continuation of Imamate. Yet this belief must not become a basis for unsupported claims about how the Imam intervenes in every event in the world or guarantees the outcome of every matter.

To understand this issue more clearly, two matters must be distinguished. The first is the Imam’s ontological and theological position within the system of divine guidance. The second concerns the extent to which people can access him directly and benefit from his leadership in a publicly visible manner.

During the period of Occultation, public access to the Imam is unavailable. Nevertheless, the Qur’an, the Sunnah of the Prophet, the teachings of the Ahl al-Bayt, and religious and ethical responsibilities remain available to humanity. Religious scholars are also responsible for explaining religion within the limits of their knowledge and competence, without their position being equated with that of the Imam.

Therefore, belief in the Hidden Imam does not mean abandoning knowledge, reason, social responsibility, or consultation of reliable religious sources. On the contrary, the deeper a person’s understanding of Imamate becomes, the clearer the need to distinguish well-founded claims from unsupported assertions.

A community awaiting the Imam is not one that accepts every statement attributed to him without examination. It is a community that distinguishes truth from claims by relying on sound and recognized religious criteria.

5. The Uprising of Imam Mahdi: The Fulfillment of the Promise of Justice

If Occultation is a period in which Imamate continues without public appearance, what place do the appearance and uprising of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) hold in human history?

According to Shi‘i belief, his appearance is not merely an extraordinary historical event or the conclusion of believers’ emotional anticipation. Rather, it represents a major stage in the fulfillment of God’s promise concerning the spread of justice, the establishment of the sovereignty of truth, and humanity’s liberation from the domination of oppression.

The Holy Qur’an states:

“We have indeed written in the Psalms, after the Reminder, that My righteous servants shall inherit the earth.” (Al-Anbiya, 21:105)

This verse speaks of a future in which God’s righteous servants will inherit the earth. In Shi‘i Mahdavi interpretation, it is among the verses considered in connection with humanity’s bright future and the rule of the righteous. However, identifying its ultimate referent requires consultation of the relevant exegetical narrations.

God also states in Surah al-Nur:

“Allah has promised those among you who believe and perform righteous deeds that He will surely make them successors on the earth … and that He will firmly establish for them the religion He has chosen for them.” (Al-Nur, 24:55)

This verse presents God’s promise of succession on earth and the establishment of religion for the righteous believers. In Mahdavi narrations and interpretations, this promise has been connected to the worldwide establishment of truth during the era of the Imam’s appearance.

Accordingly, in the Shi‘i worldview, the uprising of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) represents the fulfillment of an aspiration toward which the prophets strove: a society in which truth and justice replace oppression and corruption, and human capacities flourish in the pursuit of worshipping God and attaining genuine growth.

This uprising should not, however, be reduced to the simplistic assumption

that every problem will disappear in a single moment. Establishing global justice, transforming oppressive structures, and reforming human life constitute an immense mission that Mahdavi narrations associate with the divinely guided leadership of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him).

The grandeur of this uprising lies in the fact that justice moves beyond being an abstract ideal and becomes a horizon for organizing human and social life.

6. Waiting During the Period of Occultation: Watching for the Future or Building Oneself?

If the uprising of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) is to establish justice and the sovereignty of truth, what does awaiting his appearance mean for our lives today?

Does waiting mean merely praying for the appearance to draw near and then ignoring injustice, wrongdoing, and our own responsibilities? Can we speak of a government founded on justice while disregarding the rights of others in our personal lives?

Such an understanding of waiting is inconsistent with the purpose attributed to the Mahdavi uprising.

True waiting, before it becomes a claim about the future, is a commitment in the present. Anyone who considers themselves a believer awaiting the government of truth should strive, within their capacity, to understand the truth, uphold justice, improve their character, fulfill their social responsibilities, and raise a generation that is informed and responsible.

This does not mean that people can determine the time of the Imam’s appearance through their own efforts or independently accomplish the worldwide mission entrusted to him. The appearance is a divine promise and an act of divine will. Nevertheless, human responsibility during the period of Occultation is real and undeniable.

Recommendations published by the office of Grand Ayatollah Sayyid al-Sistani concerning the duties of believers during the Occultation emphasize knowing the Imam, expressing love for him, praying for his deliverance and appearance, and reflecting on the Imam’s distress at witnessing injustice and corruption.

From this perspective, waiting is neither passivity nor a claim to possess special communication with the Imam. Rather, it connects faith, knowledge, ethics, and responsibility.

A genuine believer awaiting the Imam prays for the arrival of justice while avoiding injustice within the sphere of their own responsibilities. Such a believer remains hopeful for the appearance but does not use that hope as an excuse to abandon their duties.

7. From the Necessity of the Imam to the Necessity of Knowing Imam Mahdi

Once the necessity of Imamate and the meaning of Occultation have been clarified, another question becomes important: Is a general belief in the Imam’s existence sufficient?

If the Imam is the Proof of God and the guide of humanity in every age, knowing him is an essential part of a believer’s theological relationship with Imamate. A person should understand whom they believe in, what his position entails, and how a divinely appointed Imam differs from claimants who attribute a special status or mission to themselves.

This issue becomes particularly important during the period of Occultation, because the lack of public access to the Imam may create conditions in which unsupported claims spread.

Therefore, love for Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) must not be separated from knowledge and careful examination. No dream, mystical vision, emotionally appealing slogan, or claim of special communication can, by itself, replace the recognized criteria for identifying the Imam and establishing the legitimacy of Imamate.

Within Shi‘i theology, knowing the Imam is connected to consulting the Qur’an, authentic Sunnah, the teachings of the Ahl al-Bayt, and evidence that can be examined and evaluated. This approach both safeguards belief in Imamate and prevents every new claim made in the name of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) from being accepted uncritically.

A community that knows its Imam must also take responsibility for understanding the truth of Imamate itself. Defending a belief without understanding its foundations cannot provide a firm basis for responding to doubts and misleading claims.

Conclusion

The necessity of an Imam in every age, the Occultation of Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him), and his worldwide uprising are not separate subjects. They are interconnected links within a unified theological framework.

Imamate represents the continuation of divine guidance after the finality of prophethood. Occultation does not mean the end of Imamate or that humanity no longer needs the Proof of God. Rather, it is a period in which the Imam of the Age exists, although his public appearance and openly manifested worldwide leadership have not yet taken place. In Shi‘i belief, the appearance marks the horizon at which God’s promise of the spread of justice and the sovereignty of truth will be fulfilled.

This connection carries a clear message for our lives today: belief in Imam Mahdi (peace be upon him) should not remain a merely formal conviction or be confined to religious ceremonies and special occasions. It should influence our understanding of religion, moral self-improvement, respect for the rights of others, social responsibility, and hope for a more just future.

The Imam is the living Proof of God in our age. Occultation is not the end of guidance, and waiting does not mean suspending human responsibility.

A community awaiting the Imam must understand his true identity, remain committed to its responsibilities during the period of Occultation, and look forward with hope and readiness to the day when the uprising of Imam Mahdi (may Allah hasten his noble return) brings the horizon of global justice into a new stage of realization.
